1Setting and notation

Consider one attention head of a pretrained Transformer. For token states xi, the head forms queries qi=WQxi and keys kj=WKxj, and scores each pair with a compatibility function. In standard attention this is the scaled dot product

sbase(qi,kj)=qi⊤kjd,Aij=softmaxj∈𝒱(i)sbase(qi,kj),

where 𝒱(i) is the set of keys that query i may attend to (its causal or padding mask). Following the framing of the project, a pretrained system exposes two adaptation targets: the representation map that produces q and k, and the relation s used to compare them. Most parameter-efficient fine-tuning changes the former, (WQ,WK)→(WQ+ΔWQ, WK+ΔWK), and leaves s unchanged.

2Compatibility-space adaptation

GeoAFT adapts the relation instead. It adds a structured, trainable compatibility residual to the base score:

Sij=sbase(qi,kj)+αhc(gϕ(Pqqi,Pkkj)−1|𝒱(i)|∑j′∈𝒱(i)gϕ(Pqqi,Pkkj′)),j∈𝒱(i),

with Sij=−∞ for j∉𝒱(i) and Aij=softmaxjSij. The components are:

  • Pq,Pk∈ℝm×d: low-dimensional projections into the space where the geometry acts;
  • gϕ: a structured geometry family with parameters ϕ, such as a metric or an exponent (see §3);
  • c>0: a fixed calibration scale, estimated once without labels in the implementation, so that the branch starts on a logit scale comparable to the base score;
  • αh: a per-head gate, initialized at 0.

The representations qi and kj are not modified by this branch. GeoAFT can be used on its own, with WQ and WK frozen, or alongside a conventional Q/K adapter. Comparing those configurations under matched budgets is the point of the experiments.

Development note. The earliest prototype mixed a bank of fixed operators per head through a router initialized at the dot product, then compiled the selection into a static model. Later versions use the gated residual above, which adds a trainable geometry instead of selecting among fixed ones and can be compared directly with matched Q/K adaptation.

3Geometry families and parameterizations

Changing the geometry changes what counts as similar. Hold one query and seven keys fixed. Under the dot product, the most compatible key is the one furthest along the query’s direction, which is not the nearest one. Euclidean distance prefers the nearest neighbour. Taxicab (L1) distance prefers neighbours along the coordinate axes. An anisotropic Mahalanobis metric prefers neighbours along a direction it treats as cheap. The winner changes every time, and no point has moved.

With u=Pqq and v=Pkk, the families used in the controlled experiments are:

The implementation also includes dot, cosine, L1, squared-L2 and rational-quadratic scores. Several of these are reducibility controls rather than candidate contributions. Cosine is a dot product after normalizing q and k. Squared L2 has an exact key-norm feature lift (§6). The scientific object is not the size of the menu. It is the question, for each family, of whether its effect could be compiled into an ordinary representation or score update.

4Identity-preserving initialization, centring and masks

Identity at initialization. With αh=0, Sij=sbase(qi,kj) for every pair, so the adapted model computes the pretrained attention exactly, up to floating-point round-off. Training therefore starts from base behaviour, and any departure is attributable to the learned residual.

Support centring. Row-wise softmax is invariant to adding a constant to all valid logits in a row:

softmaxj∈𝒱(i)(Sij+bi)=softmaxj∈𝒱(i)(Sij).

Centring the geometry branch over 𝒱(i) removes exactly this unidentifiable component. Branch scales then measure only the part of the score that can change attention. Masked positions are excluded from the mean, so centring never mixes valid and invalid keys.

Equivalence up to row constants. Two finite score matrices produce the same attention if and only if they differ by a per-row constant on the common valid support. All equivalence statements on this page are made in that sense.

5Modifying Q/K versus modifying the score

Q/K adaptation does change attention scores. It does so through the bilinear form WQ⊤WK. With low-rank updates ΔWQ=BQAQ of rank rQ and ΔWK=BKAK of rank rK,

(WQ+ΔWQ)⊤(WK+ΔWK)−WQ⊤WK=WQ⊤ΔWK+ΔWQ⊤WK+ΔWQ⊤ΔWK,

which has rank at most rQ+rK. Q/K-LoRA can only move the compatibility function within a low-rank family of bilinear corrections. A structured geometry is not restricted in this way. For N distinct points on a line, the row-centred L1 score matrix [−|xi−xj|] has rank N−1, the maximum possible after centring.

These are local statements about one head’s score matrix on fixed representations. They are not whole-Transformer impossibility results. Representations upstream can change, and a score difference can be attenuated or erased by the value and output path. A score that Q/K cannot reach is not necessarily useful for the task.

6Exact equivalences

Squared Mahalanobis. For a symmetric M=LL⊤,

−(q−k)⊤M(q−k)=2q⊤Mk−k⊤Mk−q⊤Mq.

The final term depends only on the query, so it is constant within a softmax row and cancels. A squared-Mahalanobis overlay is therefore softmax-equivalent to a member of the score family

ℱbq={q⊤Bk+k⊤Ck},B=2M,C=−M,

a bilinear term plus a key-quadratic bias. With projections, the same holds with B=2Pq⊤MPk and C=−Pk⊤MPk. Equivalently, it is a dot product in a lifted space, with the query feature (B⊤q,1) and the key feature (k,k⊤Ck).

Consequence. Near-oracle recovery of a Mahalanobis target is not evidence of capacity beyond ℱbq. The experiments include an exact control from this family, and that control outperformed the Mahalanobis overlay, which is consistent with the equivalence. Any value of the Mahalanobis parameterization has to come from economy or optimization, not from expressivity.

Other exact lifts. Squared L2 is the case M=I: −‖q−k‖2≡2q⊤k−‖k‖2 up to row constants, which is a key-norm feature lift. Cosine similarity is a dot product of normalized vectors. Neither is claimed as a new form of adaptation capacity.

7Controlled experimental design

The controlled experiments test identifiability: can a method recover a known relation? Each experiment fixes a set of representations and a target compatibility function, which defines the target attention for every query. Every method starts from the same frozen dot-product base and is trained to match the target attention. Evaluation is on held-out synthetic queries and keys.

  • Targets. A learned-Lp target, a Mahalanobis target and a nonlinear compatibility target.
  • Pairing. Methods are compared in paired cells, so wins and losses are counted cell by cell rather than only on averages.
  • Accounting. Trainable parameter counts are recorded for every method. They are counts of optimized parameters, not measurements of inference compute or memory.

A controlled success shows that a geometry family can represent and recover a relation. It does not show that the relation matters for a natural task. That question belongs to the later stages described in §11.

8Oracle-gap recovery

R=Lbase−LmethodLbase−Loracle,L=𝔼iKL(Ai·target‖Ai·method).

This is the definition used in the project protocol. Lbase is the loss of the unadapted model and Loracle is the oracle reference loss. If the oracle reproduces the target exactly, Loracle=0 and R is simply the fractional reduction in KL. R is the fraction of the available gap that a method closes: 0 means no improvement over the base, and 1 means oracle-level recovery. Normalizing by the gap makes results comparable across targets whose raw KL scales differ. Because R is a recovery fraction on a synthetic target, it should not be read as an accuracy, and it is not comparable with natural-task metrics.

Registered comparisons

Comparison rules and success margins were fixed before final testing. Not all registered superiority gates passed. Three outcomes define the boundary of the current evidence:

  • Lp target. The fixed Lp geometry bank nearly exhausted the available oracle gap, which left almost no room for adaptation to show superiority.
  • Mahalanobis target. The exact quadratic control outperformed the Mahalanobis overlay, which is consistent with the equivalence in §6.
  • Nonlinear target. GeoAFT won every paired comparison against the tested PairMLP, but the advantage fell below the preregistered materiality threshold.

Taken together, the evidence supports two of the three regimes from the overview. Exact reparameterization is established analytically, and the exact-control result is consistent with it. Economical parameterization is supported in controlled settings. Distinct adaptation capacity has not yet been demonstrated.

The three regimes

A geometry update can look different from a weight update and still compute the same function. Before asking whether geometry adaptation helps, we have to ask which kind of change it is.

  1. 1

    Exact reparameterization

    ✓ Established analytically

    The geometry change describes a function that a conventional scorer already represents. The Mahalanobis overlay is the worked case in §6.

  2. 2

    Economical parameterization

    ◐ Supported in controlled experiments

    A structured geometry may represent the same useful interaction, or a close approximation of it, with far fewer optimized parameters. On the nonlinear target, 46 trainable parameters fit about as well as the tested 8,705-parameter PairMLP.

  3. 3

    Distinct adaptation capacity

    ○ Under investigation

    Some compatibility changes may be hard for a restricted conventional family to reach (§5). Whether that difference survives the value and output path and appears in downstream tasks is the open question.

The Mahalanobis result is why this project is not about “non-dot geometry is better”. Near-perfect recovery can come from a reparameterization of a known score family.

11Limitations and open questions

Fine-tuning works within a budget of parameters, memory and optimization effort. GeoAFT turns a design default, the compatibility rule, into something that can be adapted and compared against matched conventional adaptation. Our working hypothesis is that relations matter most when representations are constrained: frozen or compressed features, small adaptation budgets, or similarity-critical tasks. Where representations are flexible, ordinary PEFT may absorb the same change. Either outcome is informative.

  • Synthetic scope. The results above recover known relations on fixed representations. They are not natural-task results, and they say nothing about state-of-the-art performance.
  • Natural tasks. Earlier natural-task experiments with previous versions of the method were negative. On hierarchical text classification and a GLUE MRPC replication, tuned Q/K-LoRA and full Q/K adaptation outperformed GeoAFT. Later coordinate-based variants did not exceed matched continued-training controls. This is why the project now asks when geometry adaptation helps rather than whether it helps in general. Broader evaluation of the current method is ongoing, and no results from it are reported here.
  • From score to task. A score change that Q/K cannot represent can still be erased by the value and output path, so non-absorbability in the score matrix does not imply task usefulness. Current work studies conditions, such as how strongly a score difference is transmitted downstream, under which the distinction should matter.
  • Efficiency. Trainable-parameter counts are not inference cost. No accuracy–compute frontier is claimed until compatible measurements of time, throughput and memory are complete.
  • Novelty boundary. Changing attention geometry and learning kernels both have precedents. The contribution under study is the regime map: when a geometry update is a reparameterization, when it is an economy, and when it is distinct.

12Engineering properties

The research implementation verifies, with tests, that zero-gated GeoAFT reproduces the base attention up to floating-point round-off. It also verifies that masked keys are excluded from centring. These are properties of the implementation. They are not evidence that geometry adaptation improves prediction.
