Part 1 · The idea

1Why adapt relations?

Inside every attention head, tokens become queries and keys, and a compatibility rule, usually a scaled dot product, decides how strongly each query attends to each key. So a pretrained model has learned two things: representations, and a rule for comparing them.

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning such as LoRA adapts the maps that produce queries and keys. The rule that compares them is inherited unchanged. GeoAFT asks what happens if we adapt that rule instead.

Our working hypothesis is that relations matter most when representations are constrained: frozen or compressed features, small adaptation budgets, or similarity-critical tasks. Where representations are flexible, ordinary fine-tuning may absorb the same change. Either outcome is informative.

2Same points, different relations

Changing the geometry changes what counts as similar. Hold one query and seven keys fixed. The dot product prefers the key furthest along the query’s direction. Euclidean distance prefers the nearest key. Taxicab (L1) distance prefers neighbours along the axes. An anisotropic Mahalanobis metric prefers neighbours along a direction it treats as cheap. The winner changes every time, and no point has moved.

3The method in one picture

GeoAFT keeps the pretrained score and adds a small, trainable geometry term on top of it:

Sij=sbase(qi,kj)+αhg¯ϕ(Pqqi,Pkkj),Aij=softmaxj∈𝒱(i)(Sij)
  • Starts from the pretrained model. Each gate αh is initialized at zero, so training begins from exactly the pretrained attention.
  • Small and structured. What is trained is the geometry itself, a metric, an exponent or a projection, not a generic learned scorer.
  • Leaves representations alone. q and k are not modified, so GeoAFT can be compared directly with matched Q/K adaptation.

Part 2 · The evidence

4Synthetic experiments controlled

We first test GeoAFT where the right answer is known. A synthetic teacher generates attention from a known compatibility function over fixed representations, and every method adapts the same frozen dot-product base toward it. The score is oracle-gap recovery R: 0% means no improvement over the base, 100% means matching the oracle. It is not classification accuracy.

5What the results mean

A geometry update can look different from a weight update and still compute the same function. So before asking whether geometry adaptation helps, we ask which kind of change it is.

  1. {{svg:assets/geoaft/fig5-regime-1.svg}}

    Exact reparameterization

    ✓ Established analytically

    The geometry describes a function a conventional scorer already represents. Mahalanobis is the example.

  2. {{svg:assets/geoaft/fig5-regime-2.svg}}

    Economical parameterization

    ◐ Supported in controlled experiments

    The same useful interaction, or a close approximation, with far fewer trained parameters.

  3. {{svg:assets/geoaft/fig5-regime-3.svg}}

    Distinct adaptation capacity

    ○ Under investigation

    Changes a restricted conventional family cannot reach. Whether this survives downstream is the open question.

The goal is a map of when adaptation should change representations and when it should change how they interact, not a claim that one geometry always wins.

Part 3 · Technical details

7Notation and definition

For token states xi, one attention head forms queries qi=WQxi and keys kj=WKxj, and scores each pair with the scaled dot product

sbase(qi,kj)=qi⊤kjd,Aij=softmaxj∈𝒱(i)sbase(qi,kj),

where 𝒱(i) is the set of keys that query i may attend to (its causal or padding mask). Most PEFT changes (WQ,WK)→(WQ+ΔWQ, WK+ΔWK) and leaves s unchanged. GeoAFT adds a structured, trainable residual to the score:

Sij=sbase(qi,kj)+αhc(gϕ(Pqqi,Pkkj)−1|𝒱(i)|∑j′∈𝒱(i)gϕ(Pqqi,Pkkj′)),j∈𝒱(i),

with Sij=−∞ for j∉𝒱(i) and Aij=softmaxjSij. Here:

  • Pq,Pk∈ℝm×d are low-dimensional projections into the space where the geometry acts;
  • gϕ is a structured geometry family with parameters ϕ, such as a metric or an exponent;
  • c>0 is a fixed calibration scale, estimated once without labels in the implementation, so the branch starts on a logit scale comparable to the base score;
  • αh is a per-head gate, initialized at 0.

GeoAFT can be used on its own, with WQ and WK frozen, or alongside a conventional Q/K adapter. Comparing those configurations under matched budgets is the point of the experiments.

Development note. The earliest prototype mixed a bank of fixed operators per head through a router initialized at the dot product, then compiled the selection into a static model. Later versions use the gated residual above, which adds a trainable geometry instead of selecting among fixed ones.

The implementation also includes dot, cosine, L1, squared-L2 and rational-quadratic scores, several of them as reducibility controls rather than candidate contributions. The size of the menu is not the contribution.

Identity at initialization. With αh=0, Sij=sbase(qi,kj) for every pair, so the adapted model computes the pretrained attention exactly, up to floating-point round-off. The research implementation verifies this with tests. This is a property of the implementation, not evidence that geometry adaptation improves prediction.

Support centring. Row-wise softmax ignores a constant added to all valid logits in a row, softmaxj∈𝒱(i)(Sij+bi)=softmaxj∈𝒱(i)(Sij). Centring the geometry branch over 𝒱(i) removes exactly this unidentifiable component. Masked positions are excluded from the mean, so centring never mixes valid and invalid keys.

9Q/K versus score adaptation

Q/K adaptation does change attention scores, through the bilinear form WQ⊤WK. With low-rank updates of rank rQ and rK,

(WQ+ΔWQ)⊤(WK+ΔWK)−WQ⊤WK=WQ⊤ΔWK+ΔWQ⊤WK+ΔWQ⊤ΔWK,

which has rank at most rQ+rK. A structured geometry is not restricted in this way: for N distinct points on a line, the row-centred L1 score matrix [−|xi−xj|] has rank N−1, the maximum possible after centring.

An exact equivalence. Not every geometry is new capacity. Squared Mahalanobis expands as

−(q−k)⊤M(q−k)=2q⊤Mk−k⊤Mk−q⊤Mq,

and the last term is constant within a softmax row, so it cancels. The overlay is softmax-equivalent to a bilinear score plus a key-quadratic bias, a known score family. Squared L2 is the case M=I, and cosine is a dot product of normalized vectors.

These are local statements about one head’s score matrix on fixed representations, not whole-Transformer impossibility results. A score that Q/K cannot reach is not necessarily useful for the task.

10Protocol, controls and full results

The controlled experiments test identifiability: can a method recover a known relation? Each fixes a set of representations and a target compatibility function. Methods are compared in paired cells on held-out synthetic queries and keys, and trainable parameter counts are recorded for every method.

R=Lbase−LmethodLbase−Loracle,L=𝔼iKL(Ai·target‖Ai·method).

Lbase is the loss of the unadapted model and Loracle is the oracle reference loss. Normalizing by the gap makes results comparable across targets whose raw KL scales differ.

11Limitations

  • Synthetic scope. The results recover known relations on fixed representations. They are not natural-task results, and they say nothing about state-of-the-art performance.
  • Natural tasks. Earlier natural-task experiments with previous versions of the method were negative. On hierarchical text classification and a GLUE MRPC replication, tuned Q/K-LoRA and full Q/K adaptation outperformed GeoAFT. Later coordinate-based variants did not exceed matched continued-training controls. This is why the project now asks when geometry adaptation helps rather than whether it helps in general.
  • From score to task. A score change that Q/K cannot represent can still be erased by the value and output path, so non-absorbability in the score matrix does not imply task usefulness.
  • Efficiency. Trainable-parameter counts are not inference cost. No accuracy–compute frontier is claimed until compatible measurements of time, throughput and memory are complete.
  • Novelty boundary. Changing attention geometry and learning kernels both have precedents. The contribution under study is the regime map.
